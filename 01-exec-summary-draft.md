## Candidate Portrait

Bruce McDougall is a technical leader who drives innovation and impact by anticipating where the network industry is headed—often three to five years ahead of mainstream adoption—and then doing the sustained work to bring Cisco, its field teams, and its largest customers there with him. His expertise spans hyperscale and cloud-native networking, AI/ML fabrics, open-source NOS (SONiC), network-as-a-service, and Segment Routing v6 (SRv6). Combined with deep service provider architecture experience, this dual SP + Web/Hyperscale specialization is unique within the PSE/DSE community and across Cisco’s sales organization.

Bruce is known as an exceptional collaborator and listener with genuine empathy for how network operators think and decide. Customers do not experience him as a vendor advocate alone; they experience him as a peer technologist who helps them solve problems of scale, agility, and architectural coherence—regardless of whether the conversation starts in a hyperscaler AI backend, a tier-1 WAN, or a global SP core. Internally, his framing of platforms, silos, air-gaps, and policy has been highly influential within the PSE/DSE organization and with multiple business units.

For nearly a decade Bruce has worked closely with Clarence Filsfils and the SR engineering organization as a field lead, trusted advisor, and innovation partner—effectively a central participant in the SR/SRv6 brain trust. He has influenced product direction, open-source strategy, and customer co-development across Cisco 8000, SONiC, IOS-XR, SD-WAN, security, and cloud-native platforms. In early 2026 he received the Pinnacle Award as part of the team recognized for SRv6 uSID’s market impact—a rare honor for a member of the sales organization.

At each stage of his career Bruce has expanded his span of influence from regional technical SME to global architect of horizon-2 and horizon-3 initiatives. He is frequently described internally as the “2030 guy”—the person teams call when the goal is to demonstrate Cisco’s vision, not merely its current product catalog.

## Becoming a Distinguished Systems Engineer *(candidate statement — first person)*

Promoting to Distinguished Systems Engineer would be the formal recognition of work I am already doing: operating at the intersection of company strategy, industry direction, and the hardest customer problems at global scale.

When I prepared my PSE package, I realized the architectures I cared about could alter Cisco’s trajectory—and the networking industry’s. That is still my goal: networks should be a delight to the operators and applications that depend on them, not a snowflake tax on every new workload. The network exists to serve; my job is to make that service scalable, programmable, and humane.

As a DSE I would:

- **For Cisco:** Continue bridging IMI, SONiC, cloud-native security (Cilium/eBPF), and SP transport into one coherent story—especially for AI factories and open NOS adoption
- **For the SE community:** Scale the lab-and-github enablement model so grade-12 SEs can co-develop with hyperscalers before products exist
- **For customers and partners:** Remain a trusted peer architect on horizon-2/3 problems—host networking, SRv6 end-to-end, MRC/multi-tenancy, and cloud-like consumption of network services
- **For the industry:** Keep publishing open artifacts (labs, emulators, architectures) that accelerate adoption and hold Cisco accountable to the future we claim

I do the hard work when nobody is looking, build credibility over years, and teach peers and customers not what is for sale today but where we need to be in three years. DSE would expand the platform from which I do that work—it would not change the mission.
