* I was an Expedia network engineer (2004-2007): in search of scaling method beyond Enterprise tools of the time: MPLS for DC?
  
* Advent of SDN / NFV (2012-2013) - mind was opened to entirely new way of thinking about networks and network architecture
  
* Segment Routing (2013-2016) - mind opened again, started exploring ideas around combining SR with SDN/NFV..first and second patent US9503363 (issued 2016), US10250494 (issued 2019)

* Cloud/host networking (2015-present) - moved to the web sales team, dug into cloud architectures. We weren't even really selling into the hyperscale/cloud DCs at the time, but I dug in anyway. In my research and customer interaction the key insight was they had moved the network control point (the overlay/PE/policy function) into the host. they did this for purposes of scale and flexibility...ToR/leaf could never provide the service scale for VMs/K8s/etc. in the cloud. Host stack was not subject to silicon limitations around policy/flexibility. Changed my entire outlook and focus. Next 10 years internally evangelizing the strategic value of Host-Networking and Cisco's need to have a technology presence there. Culminated in the Isovalent acquisition in 2023.

    * coined the phrase "host networking air-gap": The host networking air-gap – Nvidia architecture (scale-up + scale-out + DPUs), and Nvidia GPU/NIC/SiOne partnership validates the case of the importance of tying host into the network 

* SRv6 - a game changing technology that opened additional worlds of innovation, an Explosion of innovation ideas...almost all of my patents are built around applied SRv6
  * Once you’ve realized the idea of hosts or endpoints as direct consumers of SRv6 services, it’s a simple leap conceptualize other nonSP routers as consumers: sdwan, sase, TEyes, etc 
  
* AI era: another explosion of more ideas and things to work on:

    * I developed an MRC emulator as a teaching tool for SEs and customers [link](https://github.com/segmentrouting/srv6-mrc-emulator)

In the months I spent developing my PA package and working through the process towards PA promotion I realized that the kinds of things I was working on had the potential to alter the trajectory of the company, and maybe even alter the industry itself. I wanted to affect positive change in the network industry itself; to make networks and network technology become a delight to the customer, to the end user, and to applications which consume network services. And in the process, I realized, that is exactly what I want to do.
In 2012 with the early emergence of SDN and NFV, I realized the enormous potential in host-networking, of extending all of Cisco’s routing, switching, and network policy capabilities into the server where the application resides.

Our customers don’t think of themselves as “customers”, they think of themselves as network operators, or technology operators. This is an incredibly important distinction, because technology operators make decisions based on which technologies will allow them and their organization to be most successful. Business Outcomes.

Business Outcomes: the network exists to serve. Roads don’t have value unless they are used. Roads should be easy to use. For every network device running a network operating system, there are 10s, 100s, maybe 1000s of devices that run Linux, Windows, Android, iOS, etc. The Network has been a snowflake for a long time, but that is changing, and Cisco has an incredible opportunity to win that change.

I was inspired by segment routing and drove a lot of SR business 2013-2020, but became even more inspired by the power of SRv6. For nearly a decade I’ve worked very closely with Clarence Filsfils and his SR engineering team as field lead, trusted advisor, and innovation partner - I've basically been a central player in the SR/SRv6 braintrust and have had a hand or influence on every development and innovation to come from SR/SRv6 for over a decade.
