## Four Big Rocks: Repositioning Cisco for the Hyperscale and AI Era

Underneath every initiative, tiger team, and account win in this package are four architectural shifts Bruce is driving to reposition Cisco for the hyperscale and AI era. Each began as a minority position he argued years ahead of the company, and each is now a company-level initiative with a tiger team he founded or co-founded to carry it.

1. **A unified network operating system.** Cisco's hardware has converged on Silicon One, but its software remains fragmented across NX-OS, IOS-XR, and IOS-XE. Arista and Juniper sell against Cisco with a single-OS, single-management story. Bruce co-founded the **Single-Secure-OS (SSOS)** tiger team to close the gap.
   
2. **One forwarding architecture, end to end.** Cisco carries a different encapsulation in every domain. Bruce has been advocating a single forwarding architecture for years and recently co-founded the **Unified Forwarding Architecture (UFA)** SWAT team to converge on a single model, SRv6 uSID, spanning campus, WAN, DC, cloud, and into the host.
   
3. **Unified policy and identity.** Identity and segmentation are as fragmented as transport. ISE, TrustSec, SASE, and SD-WAN each carry their own version, and no service provider can reconcile a broadband subscriber with the same individual's wireless identity. Bruce recognized that a Security Group Tag and an SRv6 uSID are both 16 bits. He co-founded the **Secure Network Policy Framework (SNPF)** to carry one identity-and-policy model on UFA's forwarding plane.
   
4. **Closing the host-networking air-gap.** Bruce recognized in 2016 that hyperscale operators had moved the network's real control point into the host, and began arguing that Cisco needed a presence there; he later named the gap. Cisco's 2024 acquisition of Isovalent, which he advocated from 2021, began to close it, but Cisco still needs a SmartNIC and scale-up Ethernet for a complete AI-networking solution.

## Becoming a Distinguished Solutions Engineer *(candidate statement)*

**Who I am.** I came to Cisco as a network operator, and I have never really stopped thinking like one. What I have built since is unusual inside our sales organization: one foot in the service provider world and one in Web and hyperscale, deep enough in both that I can see where an architecture in one becomes inevitable in the other. My StandOut profile calls me a Pioneer and a Provider, and that is about right — I am drawn to the problem nobody owns yet, and I am not interested in solving it alone. The ideas in this package are mine; almost none of the outcomes are.

**Why I want this.** We have entered a new era, as an industry and arguably in human history — one of rapid and profound change, where the network is being asked to carry workloads that did not exist five years ago. Cisco has an extraordinary opportunity to thrive in it, and a real risk of arriving late. I want to be a DSE because the work that decides which of those happens is company-level work: it requires changing what Cisco builds, not just how we sell it. I have been doing that work from a Principal seat for six years. The title would match the altitude and open doors that are currently held closed.

**How I will make it happen.** The four big rocks are the plan: one network operating system, one forwarding architecture, one policy and identity model, and closing the host-networking gap all the way to the NIC. None of them can be pushed by an individual, which is why every one of them now has a tiger team and a coalition behind it rather than a slide deck. I will keep building those coalitions across business entities and across the DSE and PSE community, keep meeting operators as a peer on horizon-2 and horizon-3 problems, and keep scaling the lab-and-GitHub model so engineers can co-develop with their customers ahead of feature availability. The best outcomes in this package belong to account teams who ran with an architecture, to systems engineers who took a lab and made it theirs, and to operators who presented the idea better than I could have. That is how I intend to keep working: find people already doing good work, and give them what they need to take it further.
