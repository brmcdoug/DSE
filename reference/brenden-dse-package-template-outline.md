# Brenden Buresh DA Package — Analysis & Template Outline

This document summarizes what makes Brenden Buresh's Distinguished Architect nomination package (April 2022, 62 pages) the gold standard, and provides a reusable outline you can adapt for your own DSE candidacy.

---

## What Makes Brenden's Package Effective

### 1. A single, coherent narrative thread

Brenden's package is not a résumé. It tells one story: **an enterprise architect who expanded from area-level customer work to global influence on Cisco strategy, product direction, and field enablement** — with quantified business outcomes at every stage.

Your package should establish an equally clear through-line early. From your notes, yours might be: *the rare combination of hyperscale/cloud-native and service provider expertise, driving host-networking, SRv6, SONiC, and AI fabric architectures that alter Cisco's and the industry's trajectory.*

### 2. Summary-first, detail-second structure

Every major section opens in the Executive Overview with a **1–2 page distilled summary**, then the body sections expand with evidence. Reviewers get the argument upfront; skeptics can drill down.

### 3. Third person, factual, verifiable tone

The entire document is written in third person ("Brenden led…", "His involvement included…"). Claims are backed by:
- Dollar amounts and fiscal year breakdowns
- Attendee counts and survey scores
- Named customers, executives, and internal leaders
- Cross-references ("More details in Business Impact section")
- External evidence pointers ("Documents accessible in SharePoint repository")

### 4. The "customer problem → solution → business impact" formula

This is Brenden's most reusable pattern. Every significant entry follows:

| Element | What it does |
|--------|----------------|
| **Customer/context** | Who, industry, scale, why it mattered |
| **Problem** | Specific pain, competitive threat, or strategic gap |
| **Your role** | What *you* did (bulleted, active verbs) |
| **Outcome** | Financial, competitive, strategic, and customer impact — separately labeled |
| **Evidence** | Link, quote, recognition, or document reference |

Innovation entries add: *Customer Problem → Customer Solution → Business Impact* as explicit headings.

### 5. Layered impact — same story, different altitudes

The same accomplishments appear at three levels without feeling repetitive:
1. **Executive summary** — one paragraph + aggregate numbers ($1.65B across accounts)
2. **Global Impact / Span of Influence** — thematic summaries with cross-references
3. **Business Impact / Innovation** — full case studies (2–4 pages each for top accounts)

### 6. Tables for scanability

Brenden uses tables relentlessly and effectively:
- Career path (role, dates)
- Revenue by customer and fiscal year
- Sponsorship letters by category
- Speaking engagements, publications, affiliations
- Training sessions with dates, attendees, scores

Tables let reviewers verify breadth quickly.

### 7. Strategic + revenue impact, not just deals

The strongest entries connect field work to **company-level outcomes**: SWAT teams shaping GTM, training reaching 3,500 people, SONiC upstream commits, DNA Spaces becoming a $200M product line. Pure deal wins alone would not carry a 62-page package.

### 8. Sponsorship as proof of span, not filler

37+ letters organized by category (Direct Leadership, BE Leadership, Sales Leadership, Customer/Partner, DA/PA Community) demonstrate that influence is **validated by others at every level** — not self-reported.

### 9. SE Community Leadership as force multiplier

Brenden doesn't list mentoring as an afterthought. He quantifies: 47 EBCs in 4 years, 8 active mentees, SGM role affecting 40,000 attendees, book sprint producing a 302-page publication. Community impact is tied back to business and strategy.

### 10. Visual timeline

Page 7 includes a **multi-year timeline graphic** mapping global influence, business wins, and strategic initiatives. One image conveys a decade of compounding impact.

## Writing Mechanics That Elevate the Package

### Voice and style
- Third person throughout (except "Becoming a DSE" candidate statement)
- Active verbs: *led, drove, partnered, created, delivered, influenced*
- Avoid hedging: not "helped with" — say exactly what you owned

### SMART claims checklist
For every major claim, ensure you can answer:
- [ ] **Specific** — named customer, project, or initiative
- [ ] **Measurable** — dollars, people, scores, counts, dates
- [ ] **Attainable** — believable scope for one person (credit team where appropriate)
- [ ] **Relevant** — maps to DSE criteria category
- [ ] **Time-bound** — date range on every entry

### Evidence discipline
- [ ] Maintain a SharePoint/evidence folder; reference it consistently
- [ ] Screenshot Connected Recognition quotes verbatim
- [ ] Keep LoR PDFs organized by sponsorship category
- [ ] Link GitHub repos, blog posts, patent numbers, CL session IDs

### Cross-referencing
- Summarize in exec overview → detail once in body → don't duplicate full case studies
- Use: *"More details are located in the Business Impact section of this document."*

### What to leave out
- Technology deep-dives without business outcome
- Lists of activities without impact
- First-person throughout (save it for one section)
- Unverifiable superlatives without evidence
